## Common attack scenarios

The [Verizon 2026 DBIR overview](https://www.verizon.com/business/resources/reports/dbir/) was read. Its FAQ explicitly identifies social engineering/phishing, stolen credentials, software vulnerability exploitation and ransomware as common breach causes. Supplier compromise is included as a representative third-party risk. Categories overlap; no universal top-five ranking or unverified loss figure is claimed.

Guidance was checked against actually read CISA [email](https://github.com/cisagov/ScubaGear/blob/main/PowerShell/ScubaGear/baselines/exo.md), [identity](https://github.com/cisagov/ScubaGear/blob/main/PowerShell/ScubaGear/baselines/aad.md) and [endpoint](https://github.com/cisagov/ScubaGear/blob/main/PowerShell/ScubaGear/baselines/defender.md) baselines, plus [Known Exploited Vulnerabilities](https://github.com/cisagov/kev-data). [VERIS Community Database](https://github.com/vz-risk/VCDB) was reviewed as a public incident source; its documented sampling limitations preclude a universal ranking. CISA’s separate ransomware web guide was unavailable and is not claimed as read.

Popups give a representative problem, business impact and response/recovery/prevention measures. No invented incident is attributed to Sergey or a client.
